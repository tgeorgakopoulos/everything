This is Everything

Welcome.

An organizer for your digital life: notes, links, to-dos and reminders in one place, on the understanding that they were never really four different things.

Three steps, about five minutes.

1Make your account

Everything is invitation-only, so the door asks for the email address the invitation was sent to — that is the one to use.

Open Everything

  1. Click I have an invitation.
  2. Type the email address your invitation came to, and press Continue.
  3. Choose a password and a handle. The handle is only used if you ever publish something; it is the name on that page.

There is no confirmation email to wait for. You are in straight away, and a note called How Everything Works is waiting — it is the real tutorial, and it is written as a note so you can see the app doing the things it describes.

2Put the button in Chrome

One click. It installs like anything else from the Chrome store and keeps itself up to date.

Add Everything to Chrome

Once it is in, the E in your toolbar saves whatever page you are looking at. You can also right-click a page, a link, or text you have selected, and choose Save to Everything.

It reads nothing until you press it. There is no history of where you have been, no analytics and no trackers — pressing the button is the only thing that sends anything anywhere.

3Put it on your phone

There is nothing to install from an app store. The website becomes the app.

iPhone or iPad — open thisiseverything.eu in Safari, press the Share button at the bottom, scroll down, choose Add to Home Screen, press Add.

Android — open it in Chrome, press the ⋮ menu at the top right, choose Add to Home screen.

It gets an icon, opens without browser furniture, and stays signed in. The box at the top is the whole point of having it there: paste a link or write a thought the moment you have it, and it is on your computer by the time you sit down.

4Two things worth knowing

It opens saying “Catching up” for a second. While it does, a note you open is read-only. That is deliberate — it is checking that the version you are about to write in is the newest one, rather than something the device remembered from last week. It clears by itself.

Nothing you write is visible to anyone. Not to other users, not to us. The only exception is a note you deliberately publish, which goes to a page of your own with its own address. Everything else stays yours — Export in the settings hands you the whole library as a plain file at any moment, no permission required.

Everything is made by Social Capital, in Athens. If something is broken, confusing, or missing, say so — at this stage that is the most useful thing you can do with it.
